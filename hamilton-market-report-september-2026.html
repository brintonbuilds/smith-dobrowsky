What happened in September 2026

Overall sales declined 22.6% year over year and the median sale price fell 8.9% to $640,000. Detached homes took 44 days to sell (up 18.9%) and townhouses and condos 51 days (up 10.9%). Active inventory was down 14.5%, with 4.8 months of inventory overall — broadly balanced, but noticeably softer than a year earlier.

What it means for you

If you’re buying

Buyers have more time and more room to negotiate, with improved affordability.

Overall sales fell 22.6% year over year and the median price declined 8.9% to $640,000. Detached homes averaged 44 days on market (up 18.9%) and townhouses and condos 51 days (up 10.9%), giving buyers time to compare. Inventory is down 14.5%, and conditions can differ significantly between neighbourhoods, property types and price ranges.

If you’re selling

Reduced inventory helps, but buyers are comparing carefully, so expectations need to be realistic.

Inventory is down in both segments — detached 12.6% and condo 17.8% — which is preventing a bigger imbalance. But buyers are less inclined to overlook condition or pricing concerns, and homes are taking longer to sell. Well-presented homes priced for their neighbourhood still draw serious interest.

Common questions

Is Hamilton a buyer’s or seller’s market in September 2026?

Hamilton scored 47 out of 100 on the Smith Dobrowsky Market Balance Index™ for September 2026: balanced market, leaning toward buyers. Lower sales, softer prices and longer selling times leaned the market toward buyers, while tighter inventory kept it from tipping out of balance.

What was the median price of a detached home in Hamilton in September 2026?

The median detached sale price in Hamilton was $692,500, down 9.9% from a year earlier. The townhouse and condo median was $565,000 (down 5.8%).

How long are homes taking to sell in Hamilton?

In September 2026, detached homes in Hamilton averaged 44 days on market (up 18.9% year over year), and townhouses and condos averaged 51 days (up 10.9%).
