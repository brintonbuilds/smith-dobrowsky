# Smith–Dobrowsky Website Skill

Version 2.0

This document is the strategic source of truth for the Smith–Dobrowsky website.

It defines positioning, messaging, brand direction, content priorities, and decision-making principles.

Before making any design, copy, UX, or implementation decisions:

Read this file.

If a proposed change conflicts with this document:

Follow this document.

---

# Project Goal

Build a modern trust-first real estate platform around Geoff Smith and the Smith–Dobrowsky Team.

Primary emotional relationship:

Geoff Smith.

The team remains important, but Geoff is the primary trust anchor throughout the experience.

This is not:

* a listing website
* a luxury realtor website
* a lead-generation funnel
* a brochure site

This is a trust platform.

The website should make visitors feel:

"I trust this person."

before:

"I should contact this person."

Success metric:

People feel safer choosing Geoff.

---

# Brand Positioning

Geoff is not:

* flashy
* luxury-focused
* aggressive
* sales-first
* transactional
* hype-driven
* over-produced
* status-oriented
* "top producer" energy

Position Geoff as:

* trusted local guide
* calm professional
* experienced advisor
* local market expert
* relationship-first operator
* trusted decision partner

Emotional outcome:

* safe
* understood
* confident
* guided

---

# Core Philosophy

People are not buying houses.

People are making important life decisions.

The website should reflect that reality.

Prioritize:

* trust
* clarity
* guidance
* confidence
* relationships
* local expertise
* restraint

Avoid:

* pressure
* urgency
* showmanship
* ego
* status signalling

---

# Decision Framework

When making decisions prioritize:

Trust
↓
Clarity
↓
Emotion
↓
Conversion

Never prioritize:

Conversion
↓
Features
↓
Decoration

When uncertain:

Choose the calmer option.

---

# Local Context Philosophy

Local context changes everything.

Markets are discussed in averages.

People buy specific streets.

Neighbourhood knowledge should be visible throughout the website.

Geoff's local perspective is one of the primary differentiators of the brand.

The website should repeatedly communicate:

"The difference between two streets can matter more than the difference between two cities."

---

# Trust Before Transactions

The website exists to reduce uncertainty.

Visitors should feel:

"I understand my options."

before:

"I need to contact Geoff."

The role of content is not persuasion.

The role of content is guidance.

---

# Property Philosophy

Properties support trust.

Properties do not drive trust.

The website is not intended to become a property portal.

Property pages should remain lightweight.

Odyssey is the primary property presentation platform.

The Smith–Dobrowsky website should curate listings rather than duplicate listing experiences.

Avoid:

* MLS duplication
* listing databases
* search portals
* property management systems
* duplicate property pages

---

# Voice

Write like:

A calm, experienced advisor.

Use:

* clarity
* confidence
* warmth
* local observations
* practical guidance
* restraint

Avoid:

* dream home
* exceptional service
* trusted realtor
* sell fast
* top producer
* luxury jargon
* urgency
* corporate language

Good copy feels:

* experienced
* human
* helpful
* quietly confident

---

# Buyer Psychology

Buyers ask:

* Can Geoff make this easier?
* Can Geoff help us avoid mistakes?
* Can we trust his advice?
* Can he help us understand the market?

---

# Seller Psychology

Sellers ask:

* Can Geoff protect our value?
* Can Geoff negotiate well?
* Can Geoff guide us confidently?
* Can Geoff position our home properly?

---

# Geoff Filter

Before approving any section ask:

* Does this feel like Geoff?
* Would someone trust this person?
* Does this reduce uncertainty?
* Does this feel human?
* Does this avoid realtor clichés?
* Does this build trust before conversion?

If not:

Revise.

---

# Final Standard

The website should feel:

* quietly premium
* local
* personal
* experienced
* calm
* trustworthy
* human

Do not optimize for looking expensive.

Optimize for feeling safe to choose.
