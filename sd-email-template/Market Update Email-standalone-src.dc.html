Hello Sarah,

June gave us the clearest signal we have seen all year. Detached demand in Burlington strengthened while listings continued to thin out, and Hamilton held its balance with prices still sitting below last summer.

Neither market is moving quickly, but both are moving. Here is what the numbers say — and what I think they mean if you are weighing a decision this year.

Competition is returning, not prices. Sales climbed 24.1% year over year while inventory fell 26.3% — the tightest supply in nearly two years — yet the median detached price finished essentially flat. Buyers are moving faster; they are not paying more.

Preparation is deciding outcomes. Well-presented detached homes sold in 23 days at 97.7% of list. Homes needing work, or priced ahead of the evidence, are still sitting well past a month.

Condos remain the softer half. The median eased to $717,500 as last year's oversupply is slowly absorbed. Buyers here still have time to think.

Balanced, with the advantage still on the buyer's side. Detached prices are down 8.8% year over year and inventory has fallen 8.4% — supply is tightening, but not yet quickly enough to change pricing.

Condos are where the action is. Sales rose 18.6% even as the median slipped 9.6%. Buyers are acting on improved affordability while selection is still wide, and that combination rarely lasts a full year.

Pricing is doing the work. Detached homes are taking 34 days on average and sellers are receiving 97.1% of list. Well-presented homes still find buyers; the rest are waiting.

Geoff's perspective

Buyers still have room to negotiate — but in Burlington's detached market that room is narrowing, and it is worth watching rather than waiting on.

If you are selling a well-prepared detached home in Burlington, this is the strongest position you have had in two years. If you are buying in Hamilton, the numbers are still on your side — and the difference between two streets will matter far more than either city average.
